The quiet return of the night train

By A. Writer · 6 min read

Across Europe, sleeper trains are coming back. After decades of decline, operators are buying new carriages, reopening old routes and selling out berths weeks in advance.

Why now? Travellers say the appeal is simple. You board in one city after dinner, sleep, and wake up in another city in time for breakfast. There is no airport security line and no early alarm.

A slow comeback

The revival started in Austria, where the national railway kept its night services when others gave up. Today its network reaches Hamburg, Rome, Brussels and Amsterdam. Other companies have followed, some of them small start-ups with only a handful of trains.

Prices remain the biggest obstacle. A private compartment can cost more than a flight, and the cheapest seats are often sold months ahead. Rail advocates argue that fairer taxes on fuel would close the gap.

“It feels like travelling the way it used to be, only with better coffee,” one passenger said.

For now, the trains keep filling up. Operators expect demand to grow for years, as long as they can find enough rolling stock to meet it.
